This manual serves two crews: professional installers wiring full control sites, and DIY builders running tanks, ponds, and wells on low-voltage only. The boundary between the two is voltage — and it's a hard line.

You can do this yourself

Mounting the enclosure and board · all sensor wiring · antennas · 24 V DC power from a listed Class-2 supply · 24 V DC control loads (dosing pumps, DC valves — SLD-000 / SLD-003 patterns) · cloud setup & calibration

Licensed electrician only

Anything at 120/240 VAC: new branch circuits, panel work, line-voltage loads on CH3/CH4, contactors and well-pump circuits (SLD-001 AC portions, SLD-002). Per the NEC and most local codes, this is not optional.

Section 01 · What it isbluesignal.xyz

One box that watches the water — and acts on it.

The BlueSignal WQM-1 is a professional water quality monitoring and control unit. It measures the water, decides against your setpoints, acts through four relay outputs, and reports everything — timestamped and geotagged — to the BlueSignal Cloud. One box, one 24 V feed, one antenna mast. No PLC, no panel PC, no programming: control rules are configured from the dashboard.

What it does with the data

  1. Logs locally first — every reading is written to onboard storage (SQLite, write-ahead logging). A network outage never loses data; readings backfill automatically.
  2. Transmits over LoRaWAN — 915 MHz long-range radio, AES-128 encrypted, up to 15 km line-of-sight. No Wi-Fi, no cellular contract, no site IT dependency.
  3. Controls equipment — four independent relay outputs (NO: 10 A AC / 5 A DC · NC: 3 A) switch dosing pumps, valves, aerators, sterilizers, and alarms against cloud-configured rules (setpoint, deadband, schedule, timeout). Relay-based control is a separately priced tier with its own terms — see Section 11.
  4. Reports to cloud.bluesignal.xyz — live dashboard, alerting, exportable compliance records. Wi-Fi and LoRa units run free on the Hobbyist tier (one device); LTE units include 90 days, then a paid plan (Section 11).

Section 05 · Read firstNEC + local code govern

Safety.

  1. Disconnect all power — the 24 V DC supply AND any line voltage at the relay terminals — before opening the enclosure.
  2. Relay terminals may carry up to 250 VAC. Treat as live until verified with a meter. Line-voltage wiring must be performed by a licensed electrician per the NEC and local code.
  3. Never exceed 10 A AC / 5 A DC on NO contacts, or 3 A on NC contacts. Motors above roughly 1/3 HP go through an external contactor (DWG WQM1-SLD-002).
  4. Outdoor or wet locations require a NEMA 4X enclosure. The board is not sealed. Metallic enclosures must be bonded to the equipment grounding conductor.
  5. Chemical dosing: follow the chemical supplier's containment and injection-point instructions, and always configure a dosing timeout in the cloud.
  6. Do not power the unit with the LoRa antenna disconnected — this can damage the radio.
  7. Life-critical loads are wired fail-safe (NC). This is a rule, not an option. Any load whose loss kills stock — aeration, circulation — must be wired through NC, so that a dead controller, a crashed compute module, or a lost supply leaves the load RUNNING. Where load current exceeds the 3 A NC rating, switch a contactor coil through NC rather than the load itself (DWG WQM1-SLD-002).
  8. Control decisions execute on-device. The cloud is supervisory only and is never in the actuation path. Do not design or expect cloud-dependent control: if the link drops, on-device rules and fail-safe states continue to govern.

Hard rules

Dry contacts only · suppression on every inductive load · contactor for every motor over 1/3 HP · Class 2 DC wiring kept out of line-voltage raceways · life-critical loads on NC · never place the cloud in the actuation path. Every one of these is also a warranty condition (Section 15).

Section 06 · In the fieldPhoto every enclosure before closing

Installation methods.

Fundamentals for every install: NEMA 4X enclosure outdoors · board on M3 standoffs, connectors down · antennas outside the enclosure (LoRa vertical and high, GPS with sky view) · drip loops on every cable · interior photo uploaded to the site record before closing up.

6.1Tank / cistern (standard install)

  1. Mount the enclosure on the tank stand or adjacent wall, above the high-water line.
  2. Suspend probes at mid-depth, 300 mm minimum from walls and inlets, on a probe rack or weighted tether. The pH electrode must never be left dry on drawdown — mount below the low-water line or use a flow cell.
  3. Typical control: CH1 dosing pump at the inlet · CH2 motorized fill/divert valve · CH4 alarm.

6.2Well / pressure tank (flow-cell install)

  1. Install a flow cell (or tee manifold) on a branch off the pressure-tank outlet with isolation valves, so probes see flowing water and can be serviced without depressurizing.
  2. Keep the pH cable run under 5 m.
  3. Typical control: CH4 to a contactor in the well-pump circuit for out-of-spec shutoff · CH2 diversion valve.

6.3Aquaculture tank / pond

  1. Mount the enclosure on a post at the pond edge or tank rail, out of splash.
  2. Deploy probes off a float or fixed rack in circulating water — never the settling zone. Shade the turbidity probe; keep it clear of aeration bubbles.
  3. Typical control: CH3 aeration blower — wired fail-safe through NC per Section 05; above 3 A switch a contactor coil through NC, not the blower itself · CH1 dosing · CH4 stock-protection alarm.

Aeration is not a DO safeguard

Temperature- and schedule-driven aeration is a baseline runtime strategy, not dissolved-oxygen protection. Temperature is a weak proxy for DO: a pond can sag to lethal dissolved oxygen at dawn while temperature still reads normal. DO-critical operations require independent dissolved-oxygen monitoring and alarming. Because aeration is wired fail-safe, the failure mode to guard against is a rule that switches aeration off — never issue an aeration-off command on temperature alone, and always configure a dwell time and deadband.

6.4Freeze & seasonal care

Texas freezes are infrequent and severe — the February 2021 event destroyed a great deal of outdoor water instrumentation. Plan for it at install, not in December.

pH & RS485 probes
The glass bulb and reference junction crack when the fill solution freezes. Below freezing, either keep the probe in moving water (which resists freezing) or pull it, cap it wet with storage solution, and store above 4 °C. A probe frozen dry is a replacement, not a recalibration.
Flow cells
Drain or heat-trace any flow cell that can be isolated from flow. A frozen flow cell splits and floods the enclosure.
Enclosure
The board tolerates cold better than the probes, but condensation on thaw is the real risk. Fit a vent/drain plug at the low point and keep glands downward-facing.
Aeration on freeze
If aeration is used for de-icing, remember it is wired fail-safe NC (Section 05) — it runs on controller loss, which is the behavior you want in a freeze.
Warranty
Freeze damage to probes is normal seasonal exposure, not a defect (Section 15). Tell the customer at handoff.

6.5Distributed sites — when to add a second WQM-1

Copper has a hard ceiling. With 12 AWG (the largest wire the terminals accept) at 3% drop, a 24 VDC load tops out around 180 ft at 1 A and 90 ft at 2 A. Line-voltage loads reach further, but past roughly 60–125 ft the installed cost of trench and conduit exceeds the cost of a second unit. Beyond those limits, put a second WQM-1 at the load and link the two over LoRa instead of pulling wire.

A second unit is not a remote relay

Each WQM-1 must sense locally and decide locally. Section 05 requires that control decisions execute on-device with no network in the actuation path; that rule does not stop applying because the network is LoRa instead of the internet. A remote unit therefore needs its own probe for the parameter that drives its channel. Never wire an installation where unit A reads the water and unit B switches the load on A's command.

SituationSolutionWhy
Load within copper limits (App. B), same vesselOne unitSimplest; one probe set, one subscription.
Load beyond copper limits, but driven by the same water bodySecond unit with its own probeThe remote unit measures the same vessel at its own location and decides independently.
Second tank, pond, or wellhead with its own waterSecond unitDifferent water needs its own measurement regardless of distance.
Remote load, no local power availableCopper, or relocate the loadA unit needs 24 VDC. If you must trench for power anyway, run control with it.
Life-critical load (aeration, circulation) at distanceSecond unit, local probe, NC wiringNever place a radio link between the condition and the load that protects stock.

Commissioning a multi-unit site: claim each unit to the same site record, run the Section 13 checklist per unit, and test-fire each unit's channels independently. Each unit carries its own control-tier subscription. Cross-unit visibility is a dashboard and alerting feature — it is not a control path.

6.6Stormwater / MS4 outfall

  1. Mount above the design flood elevation; use a solar + battery 24 V supply where grid power is unavailable.
  2. Secure probes in the low-flow channel with a stainless guard; the GPS fix provides the location record for MS4 reporting.
  3. Typical control: CH4 auto-sampler trigger or telemetry alarm.

6.7Atmospheric water generation / reuse

  1. Install probes in the product-water line via flow cell, downstream of final treatment.
  2. Typical control: CH2 three-way diversion valve returning off-spec water to treatment · CH3 polish/UV loop.

Section 07 · Feed it rightVerify polarity before energizing

Power wiring.

  1. Confirm the supply is 24 V DC, 2 A minimum, and OFF.
  2. Wire + and − to the VIN terminal. 18 AWG minimum; fuse at 3 A at the supply end; keep DC runs under 10 m or upsize.
  3. Verify polarity with a meter before energizing — the input is protected, but do not rely on it.
  4. Optional: connect a 5 V enclosure fan to FAN 5V for hot locations.
  5. Do not energize until sensor, antenna, and relay wiring are complete.

Over-voltage

VIN is 24 V DC nominal. Over-voltage is the failure mode that kills boards — a mis-set bench supply or an unregulated “24 V” brick that idles high. Use a regulated, listed supply and meter it before connecting. Powering the compute module from its own USB connector is for bench work only and does not substitute for VIN. The board tolerates a wider input range than the 24 V DC it is specified to be installed with — the published warranty page states that tolerated range (9–24 V DC), and it reaches below 24 V only because the compute module's USB input is used on the bench. That is not a conflict: this section specifies what to install, the warranty states what the board survives.

7.1Grounding, bonding & surge protection

An elevated LoRa antenna is a lightning collector wired directly to the radio. In Central Texas this is the most common cause of unexplained radio failure, and surge damage is excluded from warranty (Section 15) — so the protection below is not optional advice, it is how the installation stays covered.

ElementRequirement
Mast / pole bondingBond any metallic mast, pole, or tank stand carrying the antenna to the grounding electrode system with 6 AWG CU minimum, run as straight and short as practical. Avoid sharp bends — surge does not turn corners well.
Grounding electrodeWhere no electrode is within reach (pond edge, remote outfall), drive a 5/8″ × 8 ft ground rod at the mast base and bond it back to the building's grounding electrode system per NEC 250.50 and 250.53. Two rods 6 ft apart where soil resistivity is poor. Never create an isolated ground — a second unbonded electrode makes things worse, not better.
LoRa antenna arrestorInstall a bulkhead gas-discharge-tube coaxial arrestor (SMA, 915 MHz rated) where the feedline enters the enclosure. Bond the arrestor body to the mast ground with 6 AWG. Budget its ~0.3 dB against the 3 dB loss limit in Appendix B.
GPS antenna arrestorMust be a DC-passing type. The GPS port feeds bias voltage up the coax to the active antenna, and a standard DC-blocking arrestor will kill the antenna. Verify DC continuity before energizing.
24 VDC supply sideFit a surge protective device at the panel feeding the Class 2 supply. Onboard TVS handles transients, not a direct or nearby strike.
EnclosureBond metallic enclosures to the equipment grounding conductor. Keep the antenna ground path and the EGC bonded to the same system — never to separate electrodes.

Single-point ground

Every ground in the installation (enclosure, mast, arrestors, PSU EGC) must land on one bonded system. Separate ground electrodes at different potentials will drive surge current through the board looking for the lower path. This is the failure mode that destroys otherwise-healthy units.

Grounding and bonding at line voltage is licensed-electrician work (Section 00). Have the grounding scheme reviewed as part of the electrical inspection.

Probes ship at Modbus address 1: add them one at a time via Service Window → RS485 Sensors → Scan/Add. Wiring: A yellow · B green · 12 V red (unit rail) · GND black. I²C add-on modules land on CN3, jumper-selected 3V3 or +5V.

Cable discipline

Sensor leads are signal wiring. Never bundle them with relay or line-voltage conductors; cross at 90° where paths must meet. The pH BNC lead is the most sensitive conductor on the site.

Section 09 · Close the loopSLD-000 · 001 · 002 · 003

Control wiring & the drawing set.

Monitoring and control are different products

Monitoring observes and alerts — it reads the water and tells someone. Control actuates equipment — it starts pumps, opens valves, and stops motors, with physical and biological consequences when it is wrong. The two carry different obligations.

Before any channel is enabled, a control site requires all three of: the control-tier subscription, the signed control addendum to the install agreement, and the control commissioning steps in Section 11 recorded on the Section 13 checklist. A site without these is a monitoring site — leave the relays inert.

Each channel is an isolated SPDT dry contact: the WQM-1 switches the load's own supply, it does not power the load. Route the load's supply through COM → NO (load OFF by default) or COM → NC (load ON by default / fail-safe).

Rating

NO contact: 10 A @ 250 VAC / 5 A @ 30 VDC. NC contact: 3 A. Resistive ratings (Omron G5Q-14). Inductive loads (valves, motors, contactor coils) require suppression — an MOV or RC snubber across AC loads, a flyback diode across DC coils. Unsuppressed inductive switching arcs the contacts and is excluded from warranty (Section 15). Relay coils are suppressed onboard — the requirement here is for the load side of every external circuit.

Appendix B · Size it rightDrop for copper · loss for coax

Wire gauge, voltage drop & antenna cable.

Two different problems. Copper runs fail on voltage drop long before they fail on ampacity — a 24 V circuit has only 0.72 V of headroom at the 3% design target, so DC control runs drive gauge selection. Antenna runs fail on insertion loss, which is a different calculation entirely.

Terminal limit

The VIN and relay terminals accept 18–12 AWG. If the tables below call for 10 AWG, land the heavy conductor in an adjacent junction box and pigtail 12 AWG into the board. Never force oversized wire into a 5.08 mm terminal.

The common mistake

Reglas de seguridad

  1. Desconecte toda la energía — la fuente de 24 V CC Y cualquier tensión de línea en los terminales de relé — antes de abrir la caja.
  2. Los terminales de relé pueden llevar hasta 250 V CA. Trátelos como energizados hasta verificarlo con un multímetro.
  3. Nunca exceda 10 A CA / 5 A CC en los contactos NO, ni 3 A en los contactos NC. Los motores de más de 1/3 HP requieren un contactor externo.
  4. Las cargas críticas para la vida se cablean en NC. Es una regla, no una opción. Aireación y circulación deben cablearse por NC para que un controlador muerto deje la carga FUNCIONANDO. Cuando la corriente de la carga supere los 3 A del contacto NC, conmute por NC la bobina de un contactor y no la carga misma (DWG WQM1-SLD-002).
  5. El control se ejecuta en el dispositivo. La nube es solo supervisora y nunca está en la ruta de accionamiento.
  6. Ubicaciones exteriores o húmedas requieren caja NEMA 4X / IP65. Las cajas metálicas deben conectarse al conductor de puesta a tierra.
  7. Nunca energice con la antena LoRa desconectada — puede dañar el radio.
  8. Dosificación química: configure siempre un tiempo límite de dosificación en la nube.

Reglas firmes

Solo contactos secos · supresión en toda carga inductiva · contactor para todo motor de más de 1/3 HP · cableado Clase 2 separado de canalizaciones de tensión de línea · cargas críticas en NC · nunca dependa de la nube para accionar. Todas son condiciones de la garantía.
